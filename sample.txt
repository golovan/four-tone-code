It was a quarter past nine when the telegram arrived at Larchfield Manor, and by ten o'clock everyone in the house knew that old Colonel Ashby was dead. Mrs. Pryce, the housekeeper, said it was his heart. Dr. Henley, who came over from the village in his little grey Austin, said nothing at all, which was worse.
Inspector Marlow of Scotland Yard took one look at the study and sighed. The window was locked from the inside. The fire had gone out. On the desk lay a cup of cold cocoa, a torn railway timetable and a single white glove.
"A lady's glove," said Miss Jane Whitcombe, peering over his shoulder. She was sixty-eight, wore a lavender cardigan, and had come to stay for the flower show. "Size six, I should think. But nobody in this house has hands that small, Inspector. Except, of course, the person who wants you to believe they do."
Marlow frowned. "And who might that be?"
"Oh, I couldn't possibly say," Miss Whitcombe replied, taking up her knitting again. "Not until after tea, dear."
